Let's be honest up front: "warn me before I sign" is no longer a novel idea. Several
wallets and tools already do a basic version of it. If that's all a product does, it's
one of many.

So what actually makes a security platform worth switching to? Three things that, today,
**no one offers together.**

## 1. Herd immunity, not a single company's list

Most existing warnings rely on **one vendor's database** of known-bad addresses. That's a
single point of trust — and a single point of failure.

We do it the way a colony shares danger signals. When wallets independently encounter a
new scam, those reports are pooled and confirmed by **quorum** — a threat becomes
"confirmed" only when enough *distinct* reporters agree. That does two things at once:

- **Speed:** the moment a new drainer appears, the whole network learns about it.
- **Resistance to abuse:** because it counts distinct participants, an attacker can't flood
  fake reports to either hide a scam or frame a legitimate address.

One wallet sees the trap; every wallet is warned. No central gatekeeper required.

## 2. Enforcement and decoys, not just advice

A warning you can click past is still just advice. Nature doesn't merely *warn* about an
infection — it **contains** it.

Our roadmap turns detection into action:

- **Hard-to-exit withdrawals.** Receiving funds is easy; moving large value *out* passes
  through additional checks — a design borrowed from a rotating defensive maze that's easy
  to enter and hard to escape.
- **Decoys.** When something is clearly hostile, we can route the attacker into an isolated
  copy of your wallet seeded with fake balances. They burn their effort on nothing, reveal
  their methods, and your real assets stay untouched.

## 3. Keys that can't be copied

The deepest layer is the one attackers fear most: a private key that is **bound to your
device and cannot be copied off it.** Even if malware reaches the key material, it's
useless anywhere else — and the whole system is built to stay safe against future quantum
computers, defending against "steal the encrypted data now, crack it later" attacks.

This is where the "best hardware + our software" strategy comes in: we don't reinvent the
secure chip in your phone or hardware wallet — we wrap it in active, layered defense.

## What we will never claim

We won't tell you anything is "unhackable." No honest security company should, and the
ones that do are the ones you shouldn't trust. Every claim we make is meant to be
**specific and testable**:

- A warning that reads the fine print *before* you sign.
- A scam list that's **community-verified**, not vendor-locked.
- Keys designed to be **useless if stolen.**
- Attacks that land in a **decoy**, not your real wallet.

## The path

We're shipping the simplest, highest-impact layer first — the pre-sign firewall — because
it stops the single most common way people lose crypto. Then we go deeper: herd immunity,
enforcement, decoys, and unclonable keys.

A warning is where we start. It is not where we stop.
